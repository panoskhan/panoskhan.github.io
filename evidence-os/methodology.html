METHOD & GOVERNANCE

Evidence OS Methodology

Evidence OS separates what is claimed from what is known. It records provenance so a visitor can inspect the reasoning instead of treating a page, database entry, or repeated statement as automatic proof.

1. Claim

State the proposition narrowly and preserve its original meaning. Avoid turning assumptions into facts.

2. Source

Record the exact source, publisher, date when available, and a stable link. Classify first-party, independent, academic, editorial, technical, or other source types.

3. Evidence

Explain what part of the source supports the claim. A source existing is not the same as evidence supporting a claim.

4. Verification state

  • Unverified: recorded but not sufficiently checked.
  • Supported: evidence supports the claim, but independent corroboration may be limited.
  • Verified: the project has documented a defensible verification basis appropriate to the claim.
  • Disputed: credible evidence conflicts with or materially challenges the claim.

5. Confidence

Confidence is an explicit assessment, not a mathematical probability. It must never be used to disguise missing evidence.

6. Identity verification

A shared name is not identity proof. Stronger attribution requires multiple consistent, non-sensitive signals across independent sources, such as matching professional links, projects, publications, or official profiles.

7. Provenance & change history

Records should preserve when evidence was added, revised, superseded, or challenged. Corrections should be visible rather than silently rewriting history.

8. What Evidence OS does not claim

  • It does not make the entire internet a source of truth.
  • It does not infer identity from names alone.
  • It does not manufacture third-party publications or independent corroboration.
  • It does not turn a confidence score into certainty.
